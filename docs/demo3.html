Demo 3 — Minimum Viable Product

Fully functional prototype with complete enclosure, finalized filtration, and automated control loop.

Overview

Demo 3 represents the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) milestone: a fully functional, self-contained water purification system that meets our original design specifications. The major advancements since Demo 2 include: transitioning to a new model contaminant (iron(III) oxide / rust) that better matches our filtration capabilities, completing the integrated housing assembly, and achieving a reliable automated fill → filter → sense → dispense cycle.

The system now operates as intended: dirty water enters the raw chamber, recirculates through the filtration module while the turbidity sensor monitors clarity, and is automatically diverted to the clean reservoir once the threshold is met. The user can see real-time feedback and access purified water on demand.

Key Change: Model Contaminant Switch to Iron(III) Oxide

A major development for Demo 3 was switching from diatomaceous earth (DE) to iron(III) oxide (rust, Fe₂O₃) as our model contaminant. The DE particle size distribution was too fine for our mesh-based filtration system to capture effectively at ambient pressure, making it difficult to demonstrate measurable turbidity reduction.

Why Rust Works Better Iron(III) oxide particles are larger and denser than DE, making them easier to capture with our 150 µm mesh and coffee filter combination. The rust also produces a visible color change (orange/brown → clear) that provides intuitive feedback alongside the sensor data.

Finalized Filtration Configuration

Based on extensive testing in Demo 2 and early Demo 3, we finalized a three-stage filtration approach that balances particle capture with flow rate at ambient pump pressure.

Stage 1: 150 µm Mesh Removes large debris, protects downstream stages
Stage 2: Coffee Filter (~20 µm) Captures rust particles, visible turbidity reduction
Stage 3: Activated Carbon Adsorbs taste/odor compounds, polishes water

Why This Configuration Works

  • Stage 1 (150 µm mesh): Catches large debris and prevents clogging of finer downstream filters. Allows high flow rate.
  • Stage 2 (coffee filter): The ~20 µm effective pore size captures the majority of rust particles. Provides the primary turbidity reduction that the sensor detects.
  • Stage 3 (activated carbon): Adsorbs dissolved organics, chlorine, and taste/odor compounds. Does not significantly affect turbidity but improves overall water quality.
Lesson Learned from Demo 2 The Whatman Grade 5 filter (2.5 µm) was too fine — it blocked all flow at ambient pressure. The coffee filter provides a practical middle ground: fine enough to capture rust, coarse enough to allow adequate flow from our peristaltic pump.

Application of ChemE Principles

Particle Filtration and Cake Formation

As rust particles accumulate on the coffee filter, a filter cake forms that improves capture efficiency over time. Early passes remove large particles; subsequent passes capture progressively finer material as the cake layer reduces effective pore size. This is why multi-pass recirculation improves performance even with a fixed filter medium.

Optical Turbidity Measurement

The turbidity sensor measures light transmission through the water sample. Suspended rust particles scatter and absorb light, reducing transmission. As filtration proceeds and particle concentration decreases, more light reaches the phototransistor, producing a higher voltage signal. The sensor reliably detects the transition from "dirty" to "clean" water.

Adsorption in Activated Carbon

Activated carbon removes dissolved contaminants through adsorption — molecules adhere to the carbon's high-surface-area pore structure. This targets chlorine, organic compounds, and taste/odor constituents that are not removed by particle filtration. The recirculation design increases contact time, improving adsorption efficiency.

Feedback Control System

The system implements a closed-loop control strategy: the turbidity sensor provides real-time feedback, the Arduino compares readings to a threshold, and actuates the pinch valve to divert flow when the setpoint is reached. This is the core "adaptive" behavior — the system automatically adjusts cycle time based on actual water quality rather than a fixed timer.

Pressure Drop and Flow Rate

Each filtration stage adds hydraulic resistance. The peristaltic pump operates at low pressure, so filter selection must balance capture efficiency against pressure drop. Our three-stage design was tuned to achieve adequate particle removal while maintaining usable flow rates (~50-100 mL/min).
